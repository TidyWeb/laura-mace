Where This Comes From

A practitioner whose authority is lived, not just learned.

Laura Mace did not come to this work through a textbook. She came to it through Birmingham itself — through the schools, the community centres, the families carrying wounds that statutory services rarely see.

As the mother of a son who survived a knife crime incident, Laura's motivation is personal before it is professional. ALIVE Wellness Centre CIC is the organisation she built to ensure that no family in Birmingham has to navigate that experience without support.

“She bridges the gap between formal therapeutic services and the raw reality of community life — with the credibility of someone who has sat alongside survivors.”

Laura is a trauma-informed integrative counsellor with frontline experience supporting survivors of knife crime and violence through her work at the Rape and Sexual Violence Project (RSVP). She works with individuals, couples, young people and families.

ALIVE is a registered Community Interest Company — subject to the CIC asset-lock, with community benefit obligations regulated by the CIC Regulator. It is not a pilot programme. It has been operating in Birmingham for several years, is recognised by its civic partners, and has a track record of measurable change.

Eagle Court — A Permanent Home

From mobile outreach to a permanent base.

Current Model — Mobile & Hosted

ALIVE currently delivers all services within host locations — schools, youth centres, and community hubs across Birmingham. This outreach model is effective and will continue. But it cannot provide the sustained, private, scheduled environment that the most vulnerable clients require. Those needing deeper, more focused therapeutic support have nowhere consistent to turn.

Eagle Court — Rooted & Resourced

A permanent base transforms ALIVE’s capacity entirely. Counselling delivered in-situ on a regular schedule. Activities and workshops running consistently, not subject to host availability. A safe, known address that clients, donors, partners, and commissioners can rely on. The outreach continues — and is now backed by a home.

📍

Eagle Court sits on the A45 — one of Birmingham’s principal arterial routes, served by frequent public transport links and with abundant parking. Accessible to the communities ALIVE serves across the city and the wider West Midlands.

Eagle Court — Proposed Use by Floor
Ground
Floor
Counselling Suites & Adult Education — Sound-proofed, private therapy rooms for individual, couples, and family counselling. Dedicated domestic violence sanctuary space. Multipurpose classroom and seminar rooms for adult education, vocational training, and professional development workshops. All rooms donor-funded and equipped prior to occupation.
Basement
Creative Arts & Music Production Studio — Donor-funded music production and recording studio alongside a dedicated art therapy workspace. Creative expression is a clinically evidenced component of trauma recovery. This space makes it a practical part of what ALIVE does every day. Full equipment specification provided on request.

No structural or fabric alterations required. ALIVE proposes to occupy Eagle Court as found. All fit-out is equipment-based and fully reversible. Committed donors are ready to fund and equip all spaces prior to opening. ALIVE accepts responsibility for utility costs in line with the standard Avire occupancy model.

The Challenge Here

A third of East Birmingham residents are under 16 — one of the highest proportions of children anywhere in the country. Over one in three children live in poverty. Economic inactivity runs at 35% versus 23% for England as a whole, with youth unemployment at almost twice the national average.

People living in areas of very high deprivation are three times more likely to be admitted for preventable conditions, die prematurely from preventable causes, or be in contact with mental health services. Mental health problems — from anxiety and depression through to serious conditions — are disproportionately high across the area.

6,185
knife crime incidents recorded in Birmingham across three years — over half of all West Midlands cases. Source: West Midlands Police FOI 2020–2023
Police Designation

West Midlands Police have designated adjacent Bordesley Green a Crime Impact Area, with a specific focus on tackling violence and delivering long-term improvements in community safety. This designation reflects sustained, concentrated violent crime levels placing Bordesley Green among the most acutely affected communities in the region.

This is the environment ALIVE is built for — where statutory services are stretched and community-led intervention matters most. A permanent base at Eagle Court puts ALIVE where the need is, not at a distance from it.

Governance, Compliance & Due Diligence

ALIVE Wellness Centre CIC is a properly constituted, fully compliant organisation. Every box a landlord or intermediary needs ticked is ticked.

CIC Registration & Legal Structure

ALIVE Wellness Centre CIC is registered at Companies House as a Community Interest Company — a legal structure that locks assets and surpluses for community benefit. The asset lock means no individual can extract value from the organisation. All surplus is reinvested into expanding services and accessibility.

The CIC structure gives Avire and any prospective landlord a simple assurance: ALIVE is here for its community beneficiaries, not to generate private profit.

  • Companies House compliant — no overdue filings or returns
  • Minimum 3 unrelated trustees
  • Asset lock in place per CIC regulations
  • Community Interest Statement filed and active

Safeguarding

Safeguarding is embedded at every level of ALIVE's operation. Laura Mace holds a Level 3 Designated Safeguarding Lead qualification. All staff and volunteers are DBS checked. A formal safeguarding policy is in place, reviewed regularly and available on request.

ALIVE works directly with children and young people in school and community settings. Its safeguarding framework meets the standards required by statutory partners including the NHS, West Midlands Police, and Birmingham City Council.

Equality & Diversity

ALIVE operates in full compliance with the Equality Act 2010. Its Community Interest Statement explicitly commits to inclusive, accessible provision for individuals facing barriers to traditional services — regardless of background, identity, or circumstance.

The organisation serves one of the most ethnically and culturally diverse communities in England. Equality and diversity are not policy addendums — they are operational reality.

Financial Model & Zero Capital Risk

ALIVE's proposed occupancy of Eagle Court carries zero capital risk to the landlord. The organisation proposes to occupy the building as found. All fit-out is equipment-based and fully reversible — no structural alterations required or planned.

Committed donors are ready to fund and equip all spaces prior to opening. ALIVE accepts full responsibility for utility costs in line with the standard Avire occupancy model. No rent, rates, or service charge liability falls to the landlord.

The business rates relief available to a landlord hosting a CIC tenant represents a meaningful and immediate financial benefit — with no exposure to the risks typically associated with commercial tenancies.
